The Strava source picks up activities and edits it used to miss. An activity uploaded with a start time more than a month back — history imported from another service, or a watch synced after weeks offline — is added by the daily check of the whole activity list, which used to read it and write nothing; an edit to an activity older than a month arrives the same way. A crop, a corrected distance or elevation, or a changed duration or start of a manual activity counts as an edit, so the activity's details, splits, comments, kudos and streams are fetched again. When kudos or comments arrive, the activity's comments and kudos are fetched again and its counts follow Strava's; after the update this happens once for each activity whose counts changed since it was first listed. The daily check and the six-hourly check of the last month's activities keep running while the source works through a backlog of activity details instead of waiting for it to finish, and they stop short of the rate limit as the backlog does, leaving the listing of new activities reads of its own. A Strava account that had no activities when it was connected lists every activity added later, not only the newest hundred. A bike or pair of shoes deleted on Strava now leaves the `strava_gear` table at the next weekly athlete refresh.
